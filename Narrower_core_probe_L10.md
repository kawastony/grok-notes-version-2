# Narrower-core probe at L = 10, sep = 6

Tony Kawas / 16 September 2026.

---

## Runs

Same 8-component Wilson + Callias pair, L = 10, sep = 6, with reduced core width w.

| w | softest \|λ\| | Δλ | Owner (all R) | Max P per core (R=2.5) |
|---|--------------|-----|---------------|------------------------|
| 1.2 (prior) | 0.00065 | 0.0015 | shared | ∼0.07 |
| **1.0** | **6×10⁻⁵** | **0.00022** | **shared** | ∼0.13 |
| **0.9** | **0.00031** | **0.00086** | **shared** | ∼0.14 |

Diagnostic balls R = 1.5, 2.0, 2.5 all give equal weight on both cores.

---

## Observations

1. **Soft sector survives and softens further** at narrower w (especially w = 1.0).  
2. **No preferred-core assignment** at any diagnostic radius.  
3. Absolute P_core rises modestly with larger R (as expected) but remains symmetric.  
4. IPR stays low (∼10⁻³), consistent with modes that still spread over both cores / a sizable fraction of the volume.

---

## Interpretation

Narrowing the core at fixed L = 10 and sep = 6 was not enough to exit the molecular regime. Effective separation in units of core size increased, but residual hybridization still dominates the lowest eigenstates.

This strengthens the conclusion that **true decoupling needs larger absolute separation** (and therefore L ≳ 12–16), not only a narrower profile.

## Status

| Item | Status |

| Soft topological sector | Persistent across w |

| Next low-cost options | Larger sep at L = 10 (sep = 8 if geometry fits), or accept resource limit and document |
| Decisive test | Still requires L ≳ 12 |

---

## Compact statement

Reducing core width to w = 1.0 and 0.9 at L = 10, sep = 6 keeps the soft near-zero sector and leaves the modes fully hybridized. The molecular regime is robust against this low-cost probe; independent core localization remains a larger-volume problem.
