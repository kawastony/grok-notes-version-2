# Instanton interactions — exploration

Tony Kawas / 17 September 2026.

**Scope:** Classical and ensemble interactions of instantons (I) and antiinstantons (A); molecules vs liquid; relation to the molecular hedgehog soft sector. Milestone 3 discipline: analogy allowed, false dictionary dropped.

---

## 1. Classical I–A interaction

At large separation \(R\), the interaction is a **dipole–dipole** form (orientation-dependent):

\[
S_{\rm int}
\sim
\frac{32\pi^2}{g^2}\,
\frac{\rho_I^2\rho_A^2}{R^4}
\times
(\text{orientation factor in }\eta,\bar\eta, U_{IA}).
\]

- **Attractive** for favorable relative color orientation.
- **Repulsive** for unfavorable orientation.
- Same-topology pairs (I–I or A–A): interaction vanishes at leading classical order (different story at quantum / moduli level).

### Streamline / valley

The **streamline** (Balitsky–Yung / valley) follows the gradient flow of the action from a well-separated I–A pair:

- Attractive channel: action can fall toward \(S\to 0\) (pair annihilates into perturbative fields).
- Repulsive channel: short-distance repulsion remains.
- Removes some ansatz ambiguity of sum/ratio gauges.

## 2. Molecules vs random liquid

| Regime | Structure | Physics |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Dilute random liquid** | Uncorrelated I and A | Chiral condensate from collective zero-mode hopping; topological susceptibility ~ density |
| **I–A molecules** | Tightly correlated pairs, polarized | Stronger fermionic determinant effects; relevant near \(T_c\) |
| **Chiral transition picture** | Liquid → molecular rearrangement | Polarized molecules can drive restoration and channel-dependent interactions |

Molecule-induced effective multi-fermion couplings differ in strength between scalar–pseudoscalar and vector–axial channels (lattice-compatible pattern in some models).

---

## 3. Ensemble effects of interactions

1. **Suppress large \(\rho\)** — interactions + screening cut the semiclassical infrared tail.
2. **Correlations** — pair distribution functions; not a pure ideal gas.
3. **Fermion determinant** — zero-mode overlaps between I and A generate attraction in the molecular channel and suppress random same-chirality clustering.
4. **Finite T** — molecules become more important; random liquid depleted.

## 4. Relation to *this* lattice program

### What matches at the level of analogy

| Instanton language | Hedgehog lattice (L≤10) |
|--------------------|-------------------------|
| I–A molecule | Hedgehog–antihedgehog **pair** |
| Overlapping zero modes | **Hybridized** soft modes (P₁=P₂, P_rest large) |
| Molecular vs dilute | **Molecular regime** (sep/ξ not large) |
| Orientation / chirality structure | **Mass-texture** organization (χ_βn≈−0.8) |

Our soft sector behaves like a **single topological molecule** on a small torus: shared ownership, no isolated cores, stable texture anti-alignment.

### What does not match (no dictionary)

- Classical YM streamline action ≠ Wilson–Dirac eigenvalue splitting.
- Instanton size \(\rho\) ≠ hedgehog core width \(w\) without a map.
- 4D Euclidean gauge interactions ≠ 3D mass-texture hopping on T³.
- Molecule polarization at finite T ≠ our static pair scans.

**Verdict:** Keep **molecular language** as a useful analogy for hybridization. **Drop** any claim that lattice soft splitting measures \(S_{IA}\) or streamline action.

## 5. What a lattice “interaction” study could mean here

On **this** operator (honest future work):

1. Scan **separation** more finely → splitting Δλ(sep) as effective interaction scale.  
2. Add **second pair** → soft multiplicity and whether modes cluster into independent molecules.  
3. Vary **relative orientation** of texture windings (if implementable) → analogue of I–A color orientation.  
4. Compare χ_βn and IPR in overlapping vs well-separated limits (needs larger L, residual-validated).

That studies **defect–defect interaction** in the Wilson–Dirac hedgehog system — parallel in spirit to I–A physics, not identical.

## 7. Compact statement

Instanton interactions are orientation-dependent, attractive in the I–A channel at long distance, and described by streamlines at short distance; ensembles rearrange from random liquid to molecules, especially near \(T_c\). The lattice hedgehog–antihedgehog soft sector is **molecular** in the same colloquial sense (hybridized, balanced cores). That analogy is pedagogically useful. It does not license importing streamline actions or instanton-liquid correlators as measurements of the Wilson–Dirac operator.
