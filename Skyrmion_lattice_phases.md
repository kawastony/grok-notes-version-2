# Magnetic skyrmion lattice phases

Tony Kawas / 16 September 2026.

---

## 1. Generic phase diagram of chiral cubic magnets

In non-centrosymmetric cubic magnets (MnSi, FeGe, Cu₂OSeO₃, …) competition between ferromagnetic exchange, Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI), and Zeeman energy produces a universal low-field phase sequence near the ordering temperature \(T_C\):

| Phase | Description | Typical location |
|-------|-------------|------------------|
| Helical | Single-\(q\) spiral, multi-domain | Zero / low field |
| Conical | Spiral with \(\mathbf q\parallel\mathbf H\) | Intermediate field |
| **A-phase / skyrmion lattice (SkX)** | Triple-\(q\) hexagonal (triangular) lattice of skyrmions | Narrow pocket just below \(T_C\), finite field |
| Field-polarized | Ferromagnetic alignment | High field |

The A-phase is the thermodynamically stable skyrmion *crystal*: a periodic array of topological charges with net density set by the lattice constant and the skyrmion number per unit cell (usually \(Q=\pm1\) per skyrmion).

---

## 2. Structure of the skyrmion lattice

- **Real space:** hexagonal (triangular) packing of skyrmion cores; each core is a Bloch- or Néel-type texture of winding \(\pm1\).  
- **Reciprocal space:** three (or more) fundamental helices at 120° (multi-\(q\) state) whose superposition yields the lattice.  
- **Topological density:** roughly uniform emergent field \(B^{\rm em}\propto n_{\rm Sk}\) averaged over the unit cell → topological Hall response proportional to skyrmion density.  
- **Variants:** square SkX, high-\(Q\) lattices, half-skyrmion / meron crystals, skyrmionium lattices (net \(Q=0\) per cell), metastable and light-induced SkX pockets outside the equilibrium A-phase.

---

## 3. Material examples

- **MnSi, FeGe, Fe₁₋ₓCoₓSi** — metallic B20 chiral magnets; classic A-phase near \(T_C\).  
- **Cu₂OSeO₃** — insulating chiral magnet; helical, conical, A-phase, plus additional tilted-spiral and metastable SkX regions; linear magnetoelectric coupling.  
- **Centrosymmetric / frustrated magnets** (e.g. Gd₂PdSi₃) — skyrmion lattices stabilized by geometric frustration rather than bulk DMI; often giant topological Hall effect.  
- **Interfacial thin films** — Néel-type skyrmions, room-temperature lattices, engineered DMI landscapes with tunable \(Q\).

## 4. Link to the Callias / microscopic-cone program

| Skyrmion lattice | Callias / cone lattice program |
|------------------|--------------------------------|
| Periodic array of topological charges | Periodic hedgehog–antihedgehog (or multi-defect) texture |
| Net emergent field \(\propto\) skyrmion density | Net inflow / residual set by net \(N_{\rm def}\) density |
| A-phase pocket (finite window of stability) | Soft-mode sector only when scale separation and embedding are correct |
| Multi-\(q\) superposition | Multi-winding or multi-core mass maps |
| THE of the lattice | Topology → transport asymmetry (chiral residual, galactic handedness) |
| Opposite-\(Q\) pair / skyrmionium (net zero) | Global index 0 of a compensated pair |

**Collective parallel**  
A skyrmion lattice is the many-body, continuum realisation of “many topological charges arranged so that their emergent fields add coherently.” The lattice Callias program aims at the fermionic analogue: many defect cores whose local indices add (or cancel) according to the global topology, with transport set by the residual chiral density.

**Phase-diagram parallel**  
The narrow A-phase pocket shows that topology can dominate only in a controlled window of field and temperature. Likewise, on the lattice the soft zero-mode sector appears only when the embedding is in the Callias class and the geometry (volume, separation) is adequate — outside that window the spectrum hardens or hybridizes.

## 5. What lattice phases add

1. **Collective topology** — not single defects but ordered arrays with a measurable density of charge.  
2. **Experimental phase control** — field, temperature, and thickness tune between helical, conical, SkX, and polarized states.  
3. **Transport of a lattice** — THE scales with skyrmion density; quantized regimes possible when the emergent field is large.  
4. **Metastability** — field-cooled or light-induced SkX outside the equilibrium pocket, analogous to preparing a lattice defect configuration that is not the global ground state.

---

## 6. What they do not replace

Skyrmion lattice phases are ordered spin textures (classical or itinerant). They do not compute a fermionic Callias index or protect lattice Dirac zero modes. The 8-component Wilson + isospin embedding and local diagnostics remain the correct path for a quantitative \(N_{\rm def}\) demonstration.

---

## Compact statement

Magnetic skyrmion lattice phases (the A-phase and its variants) are periodic arrays of topological spin charges stabilized by DMI or frustration in a narrow window of field and temperature. They supply the collective, continuum counterpart of a multi-defect Callias texture: density of topological charge, coherent emergent field, and topology-driven transport (THE). They strengthen the cross-scale chirality narrative without replacing the lattice index calculation; the remaining bottleneck is still geometric scale separation on the fermionic pair.
