# Fractional calculus and O_μ

## Scaffold form (Paper 24)

\[
\mathcal{O}_\mu \sim (-\nabla^2)^{-\alpha} X
\]
with dimension ratio chosen so that Δ_μ/Δ_X = 3/7 when combined with X∼Σ_b^{1/3} yields Σ_b^{1/7}.

## What fractional means here

A fractional inverse Laplacian of order α maps a source of dimension Δ_X to a field of dimension
\[
\Delta(\mathcal{O}X)=\Delta_X-2\alpha
\]
(in the usual engineering-dimension sense in d spatial dimensions the Laplacian has dimension 2). Matching Δ_μ = Δ_X − 2α to the scaffold ratio is bookkeeping unless α is fixed by the junction spectrum.

## Contact with Paper 25 geometric O_μ

Paper 25 defines O_μ via a **local** (or boundary) Legendre/transmission eigenvalue ν₀≈2.31 on the cone sector, not via an explicit fractional power of Δ.

**Honest status:**
- Fractional form = scaffold that recovers 1/7 with cubic sourcing.
- Geometric ν₀ = alternate definition of Δ_μ.
- **They are not yet shown to be the same operator.**

## Minimal consistency demand

Either:
1. Derive α from the cone junction so that Δ_X − 2α = ν₀, or
2. Drop the fractional language and use only the geometric Δ_μ=ν₀ in the γ formula, or
3. Show the transmission Green function on the sector asymptotes to a fractional kernel.

Until one of these is done, fractional calculus is **illustrative**, not the derivation of γ.
