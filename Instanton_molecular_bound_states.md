# Instanton molecular bound states

Tony Kawas / 17 September 2026.

**Scope:** I–A molecules as correlated / quasi-bound configurations; fermionic structure; role near chiral transition; analogy to lattice soft molecular sector.

---

## 1. What an “instanton molecule” is

An **instanton–antiinstanton (I–A) molecule** is a tightly correlated pair:

- Global topology cancels: \(Q_{\rm net}\approx 0\) (not a net tunneling event).
- Local topological density remains: +1 and −1 lumps nearby.
- Bound by classical attraction (favorable orientation) + fermion-induced forces.
- Not a strict quantum-mechanical bound state in all treatments — often a **strong correlation / approximate saddle** in the ensemble.

In the **streamline/valley** picture, attractive orientations can continuously deform toward annihilation (\(S\to 0\)); molecules sit at intermediate separations where correlation is strong but the pair still looks like two lumps.

---

## 2. Fermionic structure of a molecule

| Object | Single I or A | I–A molecule |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Exact zero modes | One chiral zero mode per flavor | Net index 0: **no exact zero modes** |
| Quasi-zero modes | — | Pair of opposite-chirality modes split to \(\pm\lambda\) |
| Splitting | — | \(\lambda\) decreases as separation \(\to\infty\) (recover isolated zeros) |

The would-be zero modes of I and A **hybridize** into a bonding/antibonding pair of small eigenvalues. That is the microscopic meaning of “molecular” for Dirac spectra.

This matches Banks–Casher logic at the ensemble level: a gas of isolated instantons builds a dense near-zero spectrum; a purely molecular phase depletes exact zeros and can restore chiral symmetry while still hosting topological lumps.

---

## 3. Role in the chiral transition

Standard instanton-liquid narrative:

1. **Low T:** disordered random liquid → delocalized zero modes → chiral condensate.
2. **Near \(T_c\):** growing I–A correlations → **molecular phase**.
3. **Above \(T_c\):** molecules dominate; random component depleted → chiral restoration without requiring instantons to vanish entirely.

Polarization of molecules with temperature affects the fermion determinant, pressure, and channel-dependent quark interactions (scalar–pseudoscalar often stronger than vector–axial in molecule-induced vertices).

## 4. “Bound state” language — precision

| Phrase | Accurate meaning |
|--------|------------------|
| Molecular bound state | Strongly correlated I–A pair; quasi-bound in the ensemble measure |
| Binding energy | Related to depth of streamline attraction / free-energy gain vs free pair |
| Dissociation | Pair separation \(\to\) independent I + A; soft Dirac eigenvalues \(\to\) 0 |

Not the same as a stable particle pole in Minkowski space in every formulation — the useful content is **spectral and ensemble correlation**.

## 5. Parallel to the lattice hedgehog soft sector

| Instanton molecule | Hedgehog–antihedgehog at L≤10 |
|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| Net topology ~0 | Pair with opposite windings |
| Hybridized quasi-zeros \(\pm\lambda\) | Soft cluster with splitting O(10⁻³) |
| Weight shared by both centers | **P₁ = P₂**, P_rest large |
| Isolated limit: two zeros | Expected at large sep/ξ (not yet reached) |
| Chirality structure | Ours: **texture** χ_βn≈−0.8, not plain γ₅ zeros |

**Derived on the lattice:** molecular soft sector (Milestone 1–2).  
**Analogy:** same hybridization logic as I–A molecules.  
**Not derived:** soft Δλ equals a continuum streamline binding energy.

## 8. Compact statement

Instanton molecular “bound states” are strongly correlated I–A pairs whose would-be chiral zero modes hybridize into a split quasi-zero pair; ensembles can rearrange from a random liquid into a molecular phase near the chiral transition. The Wilson–Dirac hedgehog–antihedgehog system at L≤10 realizes the same **hybridization pattern** (balanced cores, soft splitting, texture organization). That is a precise structural parallel. It is not a proof that the lattice eigenvalue is the continuum molecule binding energy.
